# trawa (trawa Energy): activities, customers, and markets

Checked on 22 November 2026. Based on public sources (website, company slides, press releases and investor posts). Figures were reported by the company or the press and have not been independently verified.

## In brief

trawa is a Berlin-based company that supplies power to businesses and adds software, control of flexible assets and its own trading desk. It targets businesses with annual power costs of at least approximately EUR 100,000. It operates in Germany and Austria and plans to enter the Netherlands in 2027.

trawa describes its mission as: “Building Europe's leading B2B energy platform.”

## Activities

trawa combines four connected activities. Partners install the hardware, including batteries, heat pumps and solar PV. trawa controls these assets.

### 1. Supply: power supply

- Renewable power for businesses, purchased against each customer's hourly load profile rather than at a standard tariff.
- A portfolio of futures and tranches for price certainty, direct wind and solar power purchase agreements (PPAs) for cost benefits, and spot-market volumes for the remainder.
- A dedicated adviser who contacts the customer when the market moves.
- Claim: approximately 10% lower power costs. The mission slide claims total savings of up to 40%.

### 2. Manage: energy portal

- Consumption, prices, costs and CO2 by location and meter.
- Cost breakdown across energy, grid fees and taxes, plus forecast costs.
- Peak-load analysis to reduce grid fees.
- One-click purchasing, either selected by the customer or based on a trawa recommendation.

### 3. Flex: asset control

- An algorithm controls batteries, solar PV, heat pumps and combined heat and power (CHP) systems, selecting the most profitable option every 15 minutes.
- Five value levers per battery: self-consumption, peak shaving, price-based charging and discharging, short-term market trading and balancing capacity.
- Claim: up to 30% additional savings on top of supply.
- Ambition: an “app store” for flexibility, in which every asset at a site can generate revenue.
- Public sources do not make clear who finances or owns the batteries.

### 4. Trading: own trading desk

- In-house traders, infrastructure and algorithms for day-ahead, intraday and futures markets.
- Execution of futures, spot and PPA volumes for customer portfolios.
- Direct marketing of third-party generation and flexibility.
- Hedging for customer price certainty, with trawa carrying the risk.

### Roadmap

trawa states: “Supply and flex today; trading, hedging, direct marketing and generation next.” The Trading slide describes the desk as already operational, so “next” may refer to standalone products. Public sources do not explain whether “generation” means owning or contracting generation assets.

## Customers

- **Target group:** industrial and commercial businesses in the SME and mid-market segments, with annual power costs of at least approximately EUR 100,000.
- **Scale:** more than 100 business customers across more than 3,000 sites in Germany.
- **Named customers:** FlixTrain, Maritim Hotels, SETEX-Textil and Conrad Electronic.
- **Sectors:** manufacturing, process industries, logistics, real estate, hospitality and retail.
- **Position:** Headline reports that trawa became the exclusive energy-procurement partner for leading industrial SME customers.
- **No households.**
- **Customer expansion:** customers later add batteries, charging points and green gas, enabling additional flexibility sales.

### Netherlands: regulatory position

- The ACM supplier licence applies to supply to small connections, up to 3x80A. Neither trawa nor Future Energy Services appeared on the ACM electricity supplier list on 22 November 2026. The gas supplier list could not be read in full.
- A supplier must either be recognised as a balance responsible party by TenneT and GTS or contract this responsibility to a recognised third party for at least one year.
- Open questions for the Netherlands profile: whether trawa will serve small business connections; whether it will become a balance responsible party or work through another party; and whether it will act as an aggregator or balancing service provider itself.

## Position and competitors

- **Revenue model:** inferred rather than published. Likely sources are a supply margin, a share of flexibility revenue, trading results and direct-marketing fees. The split is unknown.
- **Comparable model:** similar to Nomos for businesses, but without distribution through equipment manufacturers and with an in-house trading desk and procurement portfolio.
- **Closest competitors:** BEO Energy, Enerkii, Encosa, Einklang and node.energy in Germany.
- **Flexibility and trading:** Sympower in the Netherlands, Entrix, enspired and Next Kraftwerke.
- **Software for energy companies:** Powernaut, Evora and NOX.

## Unknown or unclear

- Revenue, growth, margins and volumes have not been published.
- The savings claims of 10%, 30% and 40% use different baselines.
- Ownership and financing of the batteries.
- Meaning of “generation”.
- Regulatory status in the Netherlands, as described above.
